Asynchronous Workshops

Structured participation without forcing everyone into the same room at the same time.

Asynchronous facilitation is not just a remote alternative. It is a deliberate way to give distributed teams, busy stakeholders, and quieter voices more time to reflect and contribute.

Using structured prompts, recorded guidance, and collaborative spaces, participants can work through activities in their own time while still moving toward shared understanding and clear decisions.

Tools may include:

  • Loom
  • Miro
  • FigJam
  • Notion

My Approach

Good facilitation is not about controlling a conversation.

It is about creating the conditions for the right conversation to happen.

My role is to help participants:

  • Think clearly
  • Listen deeply
  • Explore ideas safely
  • Surface assumptions
  • Navigate complexity
  • Make decisions
  • Leave with confidence and next steps

I bring a calm, structured approach that balances curiosity, empathy, and outcomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

The things teams usually want to know before booking.

What is the difference between facilitation and consulting?

Consulting often involves providing recommendations or solutions. Facilitation focuses on helping groups think together, explore challenges, and arrive at decisions through structured conversation and collaboration. Sometimes I combine both approaches depending on the needs of the engagement.

Do workshops need to be connected to product or design work?

No. While many workshops relate to products, services, or customer experiences, facilitation can also support strategic planning, organisational change, team development, and decision-making.

How many people can attend a workshop?

Sessions can be designed for small leadership groups of 4-6 people or larger groups of 30+ participants. The format and activities are tailored to the size of the group.

Do you facilitate remotely?

Yes. Most workshops can be delivered remotely using collaborative digital tools and structured activities designed specifically for distributed teams.

How long does a typical workshop last?

Workshops can range from two-hour focused sessions to multi-day engagements. Most organisations choose either a half-day or full-day format.

What happens before a workshop?

Every engagement begins with a discovery conversation. This helps clarify objectives, understand participants, identify challenges, and design the workshop around the outcomes you want to achieve.

What happens after a workshop?

Participants typically receive a summary of key insights, decisions, opportunities, and recommended next steps. Where appropriate, I can also provide facilitation notes, workshop artefacts, and follow-up support.

Do you provide workshop materials?

Yes. Any supporting materials, exercises, templates, canvases, and collaborative boards are prepared as part of the engagement.

How much does a workshop cost?

Pricing depends on the scope, duration, level of preparation required, number of participants, and whether the engagement includes research or follow-up support. The best place to start is a short discovery call.

How do I know if facilitation is the right fit?

If your team is struggling with alignment, decision-making, competing priorities, uncertainty, or complex challenges that require multiple perspectives, facilitation can often accelerate progress and create clarity. A discovery call is the easiest way to determine whether facilitation is the right approach.
